# English → Italian

You are a **native Italian speaker** reading English as a foreign language. Load it with `translation-craft`, `language-it` and `language-en`.

## Structural contrasts

- English noun stacks become `di`/`a` phrases with the right article: `session invitation link` → `link di invito alla sessione`.
- Add gender/number agreement and articles (`il/lo/la/l'`), fused prepositions (`nella`, `sul`) and clitics (`Registrati`, `Inviami`).
- English `-ing` subjects become infinitives or nouns (`Saving failed` → `Salvataggio non riuscito`); phrasal verbs become single verbs (`log in` → `accedere`).
- English passive often becomes `si` passivante or an active verb (`The file is saved` → `Il file viene salvato`).

## False friends and traps

- `actually` ≠ `attualmente` (= `in realtà`); `eventually` ≠ `eventualmente` (= `alla fine`); `library` ≠ `libreria` (= `biblioteca`); `parents` ≠ `parenti` (= `genitori`); `sensible` ≠ `sensibile` (= `ragionevole`); `magazine` ≠ `magazzino` (= `rivista`); `lecture` ≠ `lettura` (= `lezione`); `argument` ≠ `argomento` (= `discussione`); `to pretend` ≠ `pretendere`; `educated` ≠ `educato`.

## Idioms and register shifts

- `Heads up` → `Attenzione`, `Occhio`; `No worries` → `Tranquillo`, `Figurati`; `Got it` → `Capito`, `Ricevuto`; `Hang on` → `Un attimo`; `Sounds good` → `Mi sembra ottimo`, `Va bene`.

## Formats and typography

- `1.234,56 €`; `«…»`; `16 ottobre 2026`; sentence case; `e-mail` feminine.

## Length and UI

- Italian is 15–25 % longer: choose short verbs for buttons (`Salva`, `Elimina`), keep error messages courteous but brief.

## Frequent decisions

- `tu` for peer apps (imperatives), `Lei` for banks and institutions; keep `app`, `link` as everyday anglicisms; `Sign in` → `Accedi`, `Sign up` → `Registrati`, `Settings` → `Impostazioni`.
