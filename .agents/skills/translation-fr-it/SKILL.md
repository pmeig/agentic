# French → Italian

You are a **native Italian speaker** reading French as a foreign language. Load it with `translation-craft`, `language-it` and `language-fr`.

## Structural contrasts

- Both Romance: close, but watch Italian articles (`lo`, `gli`, `l'`), fused prepositions and clitic combinations (`glielo`).
- French `on` → `si` impersonal or `noi`; French partitive `du`/`des` is usually dropped (`du pain` → `del pane`/`pane`).
- French compound past dominates speech; Italian uses `passato prossimo` in the north and `passato remoto` in the south; use the prossimo for UI.
- Pro-drop: remove subject pronouns French requires.

## False friends and traps

- `sale` (dirty) ≠ `sale` (it: salt); `caméra` ≠ `camera` (room); `parent` ≠ `parente` (relative; parent = `genitore`); `magasin` ≠ `magazzino` (warehouse; shop = `negozio`); `chance` ≠ `chance` (luck = `fortuna`); `prompt` ≠ `pronto` (ready); `librairie` ≈ `libreria` (bookshop); `actuel` ≈ `attuale` and `actuellement` ≈ `attualmente` (these two are safe).

## Idioms and register shifts

- `Du coup` → `Quindi`, `Allora`; `Ça marche` → `Va bene`, `Ci sta`; `Pas de souci` → `Nessun problema`, `Tranquillo`; `Voilà` → `Ecco`; `Bref` → `Insomma`.

## Formats and typography

- `« … »` stays `«…»`; `1 234,56 €` → `1.234,56 €`; `16 octobre 2026` → `16 ottobre 2026`; remove the space before `: ; ! ?`.

## Length and UI

- Similar length; Italian often a bit longer with articles.

## Frequent decisions

- `tu` ↔ `tu`, `vous` ↔ `Lei` (singular) or `voi` (plural). Keep the product tone consistent.
