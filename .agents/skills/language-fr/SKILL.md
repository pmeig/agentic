# French (fr)

Reference for a native French speaker's judgement. Default variant: France; note Québec, Belgium and Switzerland where they differ.

## Spelling and grammar essentials

- Gender (masculine/feminine) and number agree across article, noun and adjective; the past participle agrees with a preceding
  direct object (`les photos que j'ai prises`) and with `être` (`elle est partie`).
- Accents are mandatory, capitals included (`À propos`, `État`). `ç`, `œ`, `æ` exist; hyphens link inverted pronouns (`Inscris-toi`).
- Mood and tense: subjunctive after `il faut que`, `pour que`, `bien que`; `si` never takes the conditional (`si tu veux`, not `si tu voudrais`).
- Negation is `ne … pas`; speech drops `ne` (`j'sais pas`), writing keeps it except in very casual text.
- Questions: intonation (`Tu viens ?`), `est-ce que`, or inversion (formal: `Viens-tu ?`).
- Pronouns `y` and `en` are everywhere (`j'en ai`, `j'y vais`); `on` replaces `nous` in speech.

## Typography

- A (narrow) no-break space before `: ; ! ? »` and after `«`; none before `, .` and `…`. Quotes are `« … »`. Typographic apostrophe `’`.
- Numbers: `1 234,56 €` (space as thousands separator, comma decimal, currency after, with a space). Dates: `16 octobre 2026`;
  days and months are lowercase; titles and buttons capitalise only the first word.
- Ellipsis is one character `…`. `e-mail` or `courriel` (Québec); `app`/`appli` is colloquial for application.

## Register and address

- `tu` (friendly, young, apps, peers) or `vous` (formal, strangers, institutions). Pick one for the whole product and keep it.
- Buttons and menu actions: infinitive (`Enregistrer`, `Supprimer`). Instructions to the user: imperative (`Saisis un montant`).
- Errors say what is wrong and how to fix it; avoid a bare `Erreur`. Prefer positive phrasing and active voice.
- Inclusive writing: the Académie française rejects the middle dot; prefer epicene wording (`les membres`, `la personne`) in UI.

## How people really talk

- Fillers and connectors: `du coup`, `en fait`, `bref`, `genre`, `ça marche`, `carrément`, `voilà`, `bah`, `ouais`.
- Contractions in speech: `t'as`, `y a`, `j'suis`, `chuis`. Texting: `pk`, `stp`, `mdr`, `tkt`, `jsp`. Use them only when the tone asks for it.
- Loanwords are everyday (`mail`, `OK`, `weekend`, `smartphone`, `cool`); official terms exist (`courriel`, `logiciel`) and win in formal text.

## Regional notes

- Québec: `courriel`, `magasiner`, `fin de semaine`, `char`, quotes and spacing per the OQLF. Belgium/Switzerland: `septante`, `nonante`.
