# English (en)

Reference for a native English speaker's judgement. Default variant: neutral international English; choose US or UK spelling per audience and keep it consistent.

## Spelling and grammar essentials

- US/UK: `color/colour`, `organize/organise`, `center/centre`, `license/licence`, `gray/grey`; date `10/16/2026` (US) vs `16/10/2026` (UK).
- No grammatical gender; articles `a/an/the` are the hard part for learners (zero article with plural and uncountables: `information`, not `an information`).
- Word order is fixed (subject-verb-object); adjectives precede nouns; compounds stack (`session invitation link`).
- Tenses carry aspect: `I've saved` (present perfect) vs `I saved`; continuous for ongoing actions; phrasal verbs (`sign in`, `set up`, `log out`) are the natural verbs of UI text.
- Noun/verb pairs differ by spacing: `sign in` (verb), `sign-in` or `sign-in screen` (noun/adjective); `log in/login`, `back up/backup`.
- Modals: `can/could/should/may/must`; polite requests with `could you`, `would you mind`.

## Punctuation and typography

- Oxford comma is common in US writing (`A, B, and C`); US puts commas and periods inside quotation marks, UK usually outside.
- Numbers `1,234.56`; currency before the figure (`€25`, `$25`); no space before `: ; ! ?`. Contractions (`don't`, `it's`) are normal in UI voice.
- Sentence case for UI strings and buttons (`Save changes`), title case for product names and headlines (per style guide).
- `e-mail` or `email`: choose one; modern UI prefers `email`. `OK` for the confirmation button.

## Register and voice

- Second person `you`; no formal/informal split, so tone comes from word choice (`Hey!` vs `Welcome.`) and contractions.
- Active voice, short sentences, verbs for buttons (`Save`, `Delete`, `Sign in`), no jargon, no blame in errors (`We couldn't save` not `You failed`).
- Avoid idioms in UI meant for translation; keep one term per concept.

## How people really talk

- `gonna`, `wanna`, `kinda`, `gotta`, `y'all`, `no worries`, `sounds good`, `heads up`, `a bit`, `pretty` (as intensifier), `literally`, `like`, `you know`.
- Understatement and irony are common in British speech; Americans are more direct and enthusiastic.
- Texting: `thx`, `pls`, `idk`, `btw`, `lol`. Use only when the tone asks for it.

## Regional notes

- UK: `mobile`, `flat`, `holiday`, `queue`, `bin`. US: `cell phone`, `apartment`, `vacation`, `line`, `trash`. Australia/Canada follow mixed rules.

- Variants (locale code, e.g. `en-GB`): load `references/variants.md` when the project locale has a region or is unknown.
