# German (de)

Reference for a native German speaker's judgement. Default variant: Germany; note Austria and Switzerland where they differ.

## Spelling and grammar essentials

- All nouns are capitalised and have a gender (`der`, `die`, `das`); plurals are irregular and must be learned with the noun.
- Four cases (Nominativ, Akkusativ, Dativ, Genitiv) shown by articles and adjective endings; prepositions fix the case (`mit dem Konto`, `für den Monat`, `in der Sitzung` (Dativ place) vs `in die Sitzung` (Akkusativ motion)).
- Word order: verb second in main clauses; verb last in subordinate clauses (`weil du dich registrierst`); in questions the verb comes first. Separable verbs split (`Melde dich an`, `ich melde mich an`).
- Compound nouns are written as one word (`Sitzungsname`, `Zahlungsbeleg`); use a hyphen for readability or with abbreviations (`E-Mail-Adresse`, `PIN-Code`). Gender follows the last element.
- `ß` after long vowels/diphthongs (`Straße`), `ss` after short vowels; Switzerland always writes `ss`. `ä ö ü` may become `ae oe ue` only if the system lacks them.
- Modal particles give tone: `mal`, `doch`, `halt`, `eben`, `ja`, `schon`. Perfect tense (`ich habe gespeichert`) dominates in speech over the preterite.

## Typography

- Quotes `„…“` (or `»…«`); no space before `: ; ! ?`. Numbers `1.234,56 €` (dot thousands, comma decimal, currency after with a space). Dates `16. Oktober 2026` (ordinal dot), `16.10.2026`.
- Months and weekdays are capitalised; UI labels capitalise their nouns (`Neue Sitzung`), not every word.
- `E-Mail`, `Handy` (not an English word in German), `Smartphone`; the `Link` is `der Link`.

## Register and address

- `du` (friends, young apps, most modern consumer UIs) and `Sie` (formal, institutions, older audiences). Pick one for the product and keep it, including capitalisation of `Sie/Ihr`.
- Buttons: infinitive or noun (`Speichern`, `Löschen`, `Anmelden`) is the norm; instructions to the user: imperative (`Gib einen Betrag ein`).
- Inclusive language: the Rat für deutsche Rechtschreibung does not endorse `*`/`:`/`_` within words; prefer neutral wording (`Mitglieder`, `Nutzende`, `Personen`).

## How people really talk

- `halt`, `eben`, `quasi`, `irgendwie`, `ne?`, `echt`, `krass`, `mega`, `Na ja`, `genau`, `klar`, `ach so`, `passt`.
- Anglicisms are normal in tech (`App`, `Login`, `Feedback`, `Update`), often with German verbs (`downloaden`, `liken`). In formal text prefer German terms (`herunterladen`, `Anmeldung`).
- Texting: `lg`, `vlt`, `hdl`, `mfg`, `ok`.

## Regional notes

- Austria: `Jänner`, `heuer`, `Erdäpfel`, `Sackerl`. Switzerland: `ss`, `Billett`, `Natel`, `Velo`. Standard High German is safe for a product.
