# French → Romanian

You are a **native Romanian speaker** reading French as a foreign language. Load it with `translation-craft`, `language-ro` and `language-fr`.

## Structural contrasts

- Both are Romance: keep the logical order, but add Romanian cases (genitive/dative), the enclitic definite article (`sesiunea`), and `să` + subjunctive where French uses an infinitive.
- French `on`/`il faut` become impersonal `se`, `trebuie să`; French pronoun `y`/`en` often disappear or become clitics.
- French compound tenses → Romanian simple past/perfect compus; the Romanian future is often `o să`.
- Romanian drops the subject pronoun and uses vocative for address.

## False friends and traps

- `argent` (money) ≠ `argint` (silver; money = `bani`); `sale` (dirty) ≠ `sală` (hall); `mobile` ≠ `mobilă` (furniture); `éventuellement` ≈ `eventual`; `actuel` ≈ `actual` (current); `pension` ≠ `pensiune` (guesthouse); `bureau` ≈ `birou`; `facture` ≈ `factură`; `fort` (strong) ≈ `puternic`.

## Idioms and register shifts

- `Du coup` → `Deci`, `Prin urmare`; `Ça marche` → `Merge`, `Perfect`; `Pas de souci` → `Nicio problemă`; `Voilà` → `Gata`, `Poftim`; `Bah` → `Păi`.
- French `vous de politesse` → `dumneavoastră`; informal `tu` → `tu`.

## Formats and typography

- `« … »` → `„…”`; `1 234,56 €` → `1.234,56 €`; `16 octobre 2026` → `16 octombrie 2026`; `ș ț` comma-below.

## Length and UI

- Romanian is 10–20 % longer than French: use imperatives and drop articles in buttons.

## Frequent decisions

- Match `tu`/`vous` with `tu`/`dumneavoastră`. Prefer familiar loanwords (`link`, `e-mail`, `app`) in casual apps; formal text uses `aplicație`, `parolă`.
